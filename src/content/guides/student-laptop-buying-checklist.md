This is a **desk-research buying guide**, not a claim that we personally tested specific devices or verified live listings. Exact specifications, availability and warranties should be checked with the manufacturer and seller.

## Start with what you do every week

A laptop for online classes has different demands from a machine used for virtual machines, video editing or data science notebooks. List your most demanding applications and how many you run simultaneously. Include exam software and institutional compatibility requirements, which are easy to overlook during a sale. If a course provides a prescribed operating system or specific application, check it before comparing processors.

Do not purchase purely from a model name or a dramatic percentage discount. Ask whether the exact configuration being sold has enough memory, storage and suitable ports; two laptops with nearly identical names can differ materially. Begin with a realistic maximum budget including a protective sleeve, transport, warranty options and any software you actually need.

## Memory, storage and processor

For browsing, documents and online lessons, a balanced mainstream computer may be sufficient. Programming students running an IDE, local database and container tools should consider at least 16 GB of memory if affordable; 32 GB matters mainly for heavier concurrent work. Confirm whether memory is soldered, and never assume an advertised upgrade slot exists.

Prefer solid-state storage for responsiveness. Check usable capacity, not the number on the box: the operating system, development SDKs, course videos and updates all consume room. Look for independent tests of sustained performance and cooling. An expensive dedicated graphics processor is unnecessary for most coursework unless the curriculum or personal projects need GPU acceleration.

## Comfort and repairability

The screen, keyboard and battery shape every study session. Compare display brightness for daylight use, text clarity, viewing angles and comfortable scaling. A full-size laptop may be easier for long spreadsheets, while a lighter one matters for commutes. Verify weight with its charger, not just the chassis.

Check hinges, keyboard feel, webcam and microphone quality, replaceable storage, repair policies and availability of authorized support in your city. Battery figures from a brand's marketing page may reflect a light standardized test, not a full day of browser tabs, IDE builds and video calls. Independent battery testing is worth more than a single headline number.

## Before paying

Confirm the exact processor suffix, RAM configuration, SSD capacity, display panel, ports, wireless adapter, warranty terms and return policy on the seller's listing. Make a short list of three configurations, then identify what each sacrifices. An older well-reviewed configuration can be sensible, but only if warranty and security support remain appropriate.

Avoid financing a laptop merely to reach a prestige label when a less expensive configuration meets the workload. Calculate the total installment cost and consider the value of future repairs. **Decision rule:** buy the least expensive configuration that comfortably handles your real workload, has credible service options and leaves room for a few years of updates.
