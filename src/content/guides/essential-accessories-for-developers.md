This is a **desk-research buying guide**, not a claim that we personally tested specific devices or verified live listings. Exact specifications, availability and warranties should be checked with the manufacturer and seller.

## Solve problems before shopping

Start by writing down what currently slows you down. Do you run out of ports during meetings? Does the screen sit below comfortable eye level? Are you constantly unplugging a mouse when attaching a backup drive? An accessory is useful when it addresses a repeated friction point, not because it appears in someone else's desk tour.

For a small budget, prioritize a comfortable pointing device, usable workspace lighting and a stable way to raise the display before decorative peripherals. A laptop can be a complete development machine by itself. Extra gear should make it easier to work, not impose another set of cables and drivers.

## Hub and charger compatibility

USB-C names can be misleading. A port shaped like USB-C may or may not carry DisplayPort video, high-speed data or charging input. Check the specific laptop's manual before choosing a hub. If you need an external monitor, confirm supported resolution and refresh rate, not merely a generic “4K” claim. Some docks allocate bandwidth differently depending on which ports are active.

A charger should have adequate power delivery capability for the laptop, the correct cable rating and credible electrical safety certification. Avoid unfamiliar adapters that promise unusual wattage at implausibly low prices. For many users, retaining the original laptop charger is more reliable than replacing it solely to tidy a desk.

## Keyboard, mouse and laptop stand

A comfortable keyboard depends on layout, key travel, sound and your hands. Mechanical switches are not automatically more ergonomic, and very small layouts can make shortcuts inconvenient. A mouse should allow relaxed movement without forcing your wrist into an uncomfortable angle. If working hours are long, regular movement breaks matter more than any single hardware purchase.

A stand raises the laptop screen, but creates a trade-off: typing directly on a sharply raised keyboard becomes awkward. Pair a stand with a separate keyboard when appropriate. Ensure the stand does not obstruct the cooling system or make the machine unstable. For notebooks with fragile hinges, verify a suitable opening angle.

## Storage and sensible upgrades

An external SSD is valuable for backup and transferring large development assets, but it is not a substitute for a backup strategy. Use version control for code, independent copies for irreplaceable personal data and periodic restore checks. Prefer tested cables and ports capable of sustaining the drive's claimed data rate.

Purchase in stages: first fix the biggest daily inconvenience, then work with the setup for a fortnight before adding anything else. **Decision rule:** an accessory earns its place when it measurably improves comfort, compatibility or data protection without introducing fragile dependencies.
